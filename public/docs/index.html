Using Tenwise

Tenwise is a reading workspace: follow the sources that matter, read calmly, save what's worth keeping with a note on why, and turn it into cited briefings. This guide takes you from your first sign-in to your first newsletter.

Tenwise app or Tenwise Engine? This guide is for the Tenwise app at tenwise.app. Tenwise Engine is its free, open-source core, which you run on your own computer. The engine has everything here except the features marked Tenwise app: those come with the Tenwise app.

Get started

  1. Go to tenwise.app/login (or press Sign In on this site) and enter your email.
  2. Tenwise emails you a 6-digit code and a link. Enter the code, or open the link and press Sign in. Both work once, for 15 minutes. There's no password.
  3. You land in your own library, private to your email. A short welcome helps you bring your feeds from Feedly (OPML), start from a few topics (five well-known sources each), or follow any interest: type one, such as climate tech, and pick from a Google News feed for it and matching sources. Then it helps you set up AI. You can add sources any time with + in the left rail.
  4. A Getting started card at the bottom left tracks six first steps (add your feeds, read five articles, save a highlight, try an AI summary, make a Studio draft, choose your digest time) and goes away once they're done, or when you hide it.
  5. Then a guided tour points out the article list, Saved, Collections, Studio and Help. Take it again any time from ? → Take the tour at the bottom of the left rail.

Tenwise is at its best in desktop Google Chrome 148 or later: it has Gemini Nano built in, so summaries and chat run free and private on your computer (see AI). In another browser, Tenwise says so once and offers OpenRouter's free models instead. Everything else works in any modern browser.

You stay signed in for 30 days on that browser. Signing in with a different email gives you a different, separate library.

Follow sources

Press + in the left rail (or ⌘ K → Add feed). There are several ways in:

PopularBrowse well-known sources by topic.

Already use another reader? Import your list as an OPML file in Settings → Data (or from the welcome when you first sign in); the feeds and their folders come in and start fetching right away. From Feedly: Organize → Export OPML, then import that file. Group feeds into folders from the sidebar, and right-click a feed to refresh, move or remove it. Tenwise refreshes your feeds while you're using it: set how often in Settings → Reading, or press ↻ in the left rail to refresh them all now. Tenwise app During your trial and with Tenwise Plus, your feeds also refresh every few hours while you're away, so new articles are waiting when you come back.

Read

  • Scan fast. The list shows dense rows with day headings. Prefer something else? Switch to Grid, List or Compact in the list header.
  • Read calmly. Open an article and it docks beside the list. Tenwise fetches the full text from the source when it can, and tells you when you're reading only the feed's summary.
  • Make room. Hide the sidebar with [ and the list with ], or press f for focus mode. The Aa menu sets font, size, spacing and width.
  • Highlight. Select text in an article to highlight it in one of four colors. Highlighting an article also saves it, so it isn't lost under tomorrow's articles. Find every highlight in Highlights.
  • Mute and alert. In Settings → Reading → Mute and alert rules, list words or feeds to mute: matching articles disappear from every view and count (your saves keep what you saved). List alert words, and matching articles are marked in the list. Words match whole words, so muting "ai" doesn't hide "said".
  • Since your last visitTenwise app: when you come back, a card says how many new articles arrived while you were away, and where.
  • Daily digestTenwise app: an email each morning with the new articles from your feeds, at the hour you choose in Settings → Account → Email. Each article opens straight in Tenwise.
  • Find anything. Press / to search everything you've fetched, or ⌘ K to jump to any view, folder or feed and run commands.

Save and organize

  • b saves an article. n saves it and opens a note: a line on why it caught your eye. Saved lists your saves newest first, each with its note and how many highlights it has. Un-saving an article keeps its highlights.
  • c files an article into collections, or creates one as you type. A collection is a set of sources on a topic, ready to cite or share.
  • Unsorted holds saves that aren't in a collection yet: a filing queue. File one with c, and j takes you to the next.
  • Removing a feed never removes your saves: they stay in Saved and your collections, notes included.

Cite and export

  • Every saved article carries an APA 7 citation: under your note in the reader, and behind a copy button on saved rows. It pastes into a document with its italics.
  • A collection's Copy citations copies a reference list, A to Z.
  • Download Markdown saves a collection as one file: each article's title, source, dates, citation, your note and its text.
  • Send to NotebookLMTenwise app on a collection downloads that file to upload as a source, copies the links to paste, or opens NotebookLM.
  • Export your feed list as OPML (Settings → Data), and your highlights as CSV from Highlights.

AI

Tenwise works with three kinds of AI. Pick whichever fits; you can change it any time in Settings → AI engines.

  • Gemini Nano in Chrome (free, private): the model built into desktop Google Chrome runs in your browser, so article text isn't sent to an AI service. Set-up is below.
  • OpenRouter's free modelsTenwise app (free, any browser): in Settings → AI engines → Your own AI key, choose OpenRouter (free models), follow the link to get a free key at openrouter.ai, paste it, and pick a model marked free. Free models have daily limits, and some free providers may use what you send them to train models, so don't send private text through them.
  • Your own keyTenwise app (any browser): Claude, OpenAI, OpenRouter or another OpenAI-compatible service, with your own API key. The key is checked with the provider, stored encrypted, and never shown again; the article text you ask about goes to that provider.

Gemini Nano in Chrome

  1. Use desktop Chrome 148 or later. Chrome needs about 22 GB of free disk space and a computer it supports; chrome://on-device-internals shows whether yours qualifies.
  2. Open an article and press a for the AI rail.
  3. Press Generate summary. The first time, Chrome downloads Gemini Nano once (a few GB); after that it's ready whenever you are.

The AI rail has Summary, AnalysisTenwise app (sentiment, urgency, frame, tone, depth and topics), Chat about the article, and your Highlights. Save a summary to keep it in AI Summaries.

Your voiceTenwise app

In Settings → AI assistant, choose reading personas (several blend into one voice), a tone, and custom instructions. They apply to every summary, chat and briefing.

Background processingTenwise app

Turn it on in Settings → AI engines to summarize and analyze new articles automatically while Tenwise is open in Chrome, or press ✨ in the list header to process recent articles now. Analyzed articles can then be filtered by sentiment, urgency and more with the Filters button.

Ollama, the other AI engine the open-source Tenwise Engine supports, needs Tenwise to run on your own computer, so it isn't available on tenwise.app.

Export the result as Markdown or Word, email it, or save a newsletter to Studio.

Studio newslettersTenwise app

Studio (in the left rail) keeps your newsletters. Save one from a generated newsletter, or start a draft from any collection: each article becomes a section, with your note as "Why it matters" and a reference list at the end.

  • Edit the title and Markdown with a live preview; changes save as you type.
  • Pick a design: Broadsheet, Evening Edition or Quarterly.
  • Export as Markdown, Word, a PNG for sharing, or an A4 PDF with real text.

Make it yours

  • Theme: dark or light, from the sun and moon in the left rail or Settings → Appearance. The sign-in page has its own switch, and starts light.
  • Accent color, fonts and text color: Settings → Appearance.
  • Simple and Power: Settings opens in Simple, with what most people need. Power shows everything, including the AI prompts, which you can read and edit.
  • Your settings follow you to any browser you sign in on.

Your account and dataTenwise app

  • Your account in one place: click your initial at the bottom of the left rail, then Account settings or Plan & billing. Both open Settings → Account.
  • Plan & billing: your Tenwise Plus plan or how many trial days are left. Subscribe takes you to Stripe to pay; Manage billing opens Stripe's page to change your card, see invoices or cancel. If you cancel, you keep Tenwise Plus until the end of the period you paid for.
  • Email: turn the daily digest, tips and product news on or off, and choose the hour the digest comes, in your own time zone. Emails about your account, your trial and billing always come. Every email also has a one-click unsubscribe.
  • Sign out: from the same menu, or Settings → Account. You can sign out on every device at once.
  • Download your library: Settings → Data → Download your library gives you one file with your feeds, folders, saves, highlights, notes, collections, AI summaries and Studio drafts, on any plan, read-only included. It opens in the free, open-source Tenwise Engine on your own computer, so your library is always yours.
  • Your library is private to your email: nobody else signing in can see your feeds, saves or notes.
  • Where it's kept: your library lives in your own workspace on Tenwise's servers (on Cloudflare), with a continuous backup, so it's there on any browser you sign in on.
  • Where AI runs: Gemini Nano stays on your device. With your own key or OpenRouter, the text you ask about goes to that provider, and nowhere else.
  • No tracking: no third-party analytics or advertising.

If something's not right

The sign-in email didn't arrive

Check spam, then request a new one; each new email replaces the last. To stop abuse, Tenwise sends only a few sign-in emails per address in a short time, so if you're told to wait, wait a few minutes.

"That code isn't right" or "expired"

Codes work once, for 15 minutes, and five wrong tries end them. Request a new code.

"No AI model available"

Open Tenwise in desktop Google Chrome 148 or later. If it still says so, chrome://on-device-internals shows why Chrome can't run Gemini Nano on that computer.

A feed won't add

Some sites block automated requests. Try the feed's own address instead of the site's home page.

The full article doesn't load

The site blocked it. Press o to read it on the site.

Anything else: write to satish@tenwise.app.
